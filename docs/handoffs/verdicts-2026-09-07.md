# Handoff: verdicts on the rows that are not live

Written 2026-09-07 by the orchestrating session for an Opus 5 session to run.
Read `CLAUDE.md` first, all of it. Then the timing, amounts and programmes
briefs in this folder and their results files: every rule and trap there
applies, and the programmes brief's four tests are the bar here too. Then this.

## The job in one sentence

For every row that is not live, read the funder's page today and write one
verdict with the page's own sentence: publish, reject, or park. Tidy the row
so that a publish needs nothing more than Paul's click. Paul decides from the
list; **nothing in this job goes live**, and nothing is rejected by you.

The rows are in `docs/handoffs/verdict-rows-2026-09-07.json`, two piles:

- **Pile A, 67 rows in review that have never been live** (`tagged`,
  `tagged_awaiting_review`, `captured`). Do these first, in file order,
  batches of 15.
- **Pile B, 195 rows that were live and are hidden now** (`published` with
  `is_active` false). Mostly expired deadlines. The question is narrower: has
  the fund reopened, or has it a stated next date? Batches of 25.

## The verdicts

| verdict | when | what you write on the row |
|---|---|---|
| `publish` | passes all four tests from the programmes brief (open to organisations, open or opening on a stated date, gives something, the page says who can apply), and the row's timing and amount fields agree with the page | the row tidied: timing per the timing brief, amount per the amounts brief, `who_can_apply` and `exclusions` in the brief with quotes, `apply_url` on the fund's own page. State unchanged. |
| `park` | real fund, closed now, page states when it reopens | `next_open_date` and `next_open_date_parsed` from the page, per the timing brief; state unchanged |
| `reject` | fails a test: individuals only, invitation only, nothing to apply for, a directory or a news item, fund ended, a duplicate of a live row (name the id) | nothing on the row; the verdict carries the reject code from `src/lib/admin/reject-reasons.ts` and the sentence |
| `hold` | you cannot tell: page unreadable, eligibility unstated, or the answer needs a judgement Paul has to make (a pin of his that the page contradicts, an audience call at the edge) | nothing on the row; say exactly what Paul needs to look at |

A verdict without a verbatim sentence from the page is a `hold`, with one
exception: a duplicate proved in the database is a `reject` on that evidence
alone, the live row's id and the matching host or title standing in for the
sentence (ruled 7 Sept, pile A batch 3). A `publish`
on a row whose timing or amount you could not confirm is a `hold`.

## A `publish` needs a full brief, written from the page

Paul, 7 Sept: rows go live without being enriched, and that keeps happening.
So a `publish` verdict is only allowed when the row's `funder_brief` is
complete **to depth**, and you write the missing parts yourself from the page
(no enrich endpoint, no API). Complete means every one of these, each two or
three plain sentences in the catalogue's voice, each with a verbatim citation
and `source_url`:

- `who_can_apply`: organisation types, place, income limits, any conditions
- `what_they_fund`: the work, the costs (core, project, capital), the priorities
- `how_to_apply`: the route (form, portal, email, expression of interest), the steps
- `exclusions`: what the page rules out; if the page states none, write
  "The page states no exclusions" and cite the closest sentence
- `decision_timeline`: rounds, panel dates, how long to a decision
- `typical_award`: the per-applicant figure or the honest prose version
  (average, rate, share of income, strand ceilings) per the amounts brief
- `open_status`: `open`, `between_rounds` or `closed`, agreeing with the timing fields

Where the page genuinely says nothing about one of these (a lender with no
decision timeline, a fund with no exclusions), write "The page states no
decision timeline" (or the equivalent) and cite the closest sentence, as for
exclusions. Six fields from the page plus one honest absence is complete;
"not stated" where the page does state it is not. Ruled 7 Sept on Charity
Bank Green Loans.

A brief that has the field but says "not stated" or "see website" where the
page does say it is NOT complete. Test it as the review gate does: does the
brief tell a fundraiser more than the card already shows. If the page cannot
support a complete brief (a homepage, an index over many funds), the verdict
is `hold` with the reason, not `publish`.

Write the brief with `mergeGrantUpdate` on `funder_brief`, `source: 'live_fetch'`,
`last_enriched` today, and `_citations` carrying every field's quote, in the
shape `scripts/newsletter-batch-2026-09-04.ts` uses. List every field you
wrote under `tidied` in the verdict.

## Rules that are not optional

1. **Nothing changes state.** No `is_active`, no `pipeline_state`, no
   `rejection_reason`. Those are Paul's, from the review queue. The verdict
   file is the only place a reject or a publish exists until he acts.
2. **Trust of the writes.** Anything read off the page today, with the
   sentence as its citation, is written at `user_verified:verdicts-2026-09-07`
   (trust 70) in both piles: the brief, timing, amounts, `apply_url`. That
   outranks the `ai_enrich:v2` briefs (trust 60) most pile A rows already
   carry, which is the point: those briefs are what Paul means by "not
   enriched", and a cited brief from today's page must replace them. A later
   Re-enrich will be refused on those fields, and the review screen shows the
   refusal, which is the right behaviour for a field a person has verified
   against the page. `system:` trust (50) is only for a value you inferred
   rather than read (a location tag from an address, a sector). Never
   `admin:`. Decided 7 Sept after batch 1, where a system-trust brief was
   refused on 57 of 67 rows.
3. **Every write carries the page's sentence**, with the date or the number
   in it, `source_url` on every citation. The checker confirms quotes.
4. **A `needs_intervention_reason` note is a tombstone** (CLAUDE.md): a row carrying
   one is frozen out of every cron. If the reason is resolved, say so in the
   verdict and Paul clears it; do not clear it yourself.
5. **Admin-held fields are reported, not overwritten.** The JSON lists them
   per row. If the page contradicts one, the verdict is `hold` with both
   sentences.
6. **Read with node's fetch and a browser user agent**, never curl; an empty
   body or headings with nothing under them is unread. No Anthropic API
   spend: no `@anthropic-ai/sdk`, no `/api/admin/enrich-grant`. If a row
   has no brief at all and the page is readable, write `who_can_apply`,
   `what_they_fund`, `how_to_apply` and `exclusions` yourself from the page,
   two or three plain sentences each, with citations; that is the tidy.
7. **Dedup every `publish` in SQL** against the whole table by host, title
   words and funder before the verdict is written; a duplicate is a `reject`
   naming the live id.
8. **Dry run, `npx tsc --noEmit`, commit after every batch**, results file
   and script together, push to `main`, message grant-tracker-be after each
   push with the batch's verdict counts and every `hold`.

## Results file

`docs/handoffs/verdict-results-2026-09-07.json`, a dict with `batches` and a
closing `summary`. Each verdict:

```json
{ "id": "…", "title": "…", "pile": "A|B", "verdict": "publish|park|reject|hold",
  "code": "<reject code, reject only>", "quote": "…", "url": "…",
  "tidied": ["deadline", "funder_brief.who_can_apply"],
  "for_paul": "one sentence: what he is deciding and why it is his call (hold and reject only)" }
```

Append a `paul_list` at the end of each batch: the verdicts as one line each,
`publish` first, then `park`, `reject`, `hold`, so he can work down it.

## What the orchestrating session checks afterwards

A sample of `publish` verdicts re-read against the four tests and the row's
fields; the checker run on every tidied row; the dedup query over every
`publish` title and host; the count of rows whose state changed during the
job, which must be zero. Then the list goes to Paul.

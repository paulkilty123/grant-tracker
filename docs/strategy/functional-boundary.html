The organising principle: who does the work

Free — you do the work. You search, scan a fit-ranked list, check the verdicts, decide, and track. Everything you see is verified and honest: real links, real deadlines, no blurring, no locked teaser cards. The free tier must beat the free incumbents (360Giving, GrantFinder, My Funding Central) on data quality and matching — it is the acquisition engine and the funnel the conversion maths depends on, and it matches what the MCP already demonstrates for free.

Paid — the assistant does the work. It holds your goal, reasons across your whole situation, sequences and prioritises with explanations, runs the audit-grade eligibility breakdowns, watches your pipeline and pacing, reaches out only when it matters, and learns from every correction. The step-up is categorical (judgment, vigilance, execution relief) — not a search filter unlocked.

The bridge — the 14-day trial. Every free user can experience the full companion once. Conversion comes from feeling the step-up, never from engineered friction in the free tier.

The trial — the bridge across the boundary

Entry

One tap from anywhere

Any free user starts the 14-day companion trial by setting a goal (the wizard). No card upfront. The first populated dashboard is the conversion moment — first-week time-to-value is the strongest predictor.

During

The full Lighter Companion

Everything in the Lighter column, for real, on their real situation. Instrumented from day one (first-run telemetry pulled forward in the build plan).

Expiry

Graceful, honest, reversible

Agent features pause; nothing is deleted. Their goal, learned context and pipeline are retained and reactivate on upgrade. Clear summary of what the agent did during the trial (time saved, actions surfaced). No nagging — one clear offer, then quiet.

Boundary rules — so the line never drifts into dark patterns

The free tier is complete, not crippled. Every free card is a finished, honest unit: verified facts, real links, real deadlines, an honest verdict. Nothing on a free card is blurred, locked, or replaced by an upsell placeholder. The paid tier is experienced as addition (a brilliant colleague joins), never as the removal of friction that was put there on purpose.
The verdict/audit line on eligibility. Free users always see whether something fits (the verdict — anything else would make free matching dishonest). Paid users see the audit-grade why, with citations. This monetises the genuinely defensible piece (the verified eligibility engine) without hobbling acquisition or trust.
Future features placed by "who does the work." Any new capability where the user operates it → free candidate. Any capability where the assistant exercises judgment, vigilance or execution on the user's behalf → paid. This is the test that keeps the boundary coherent as the product grows.
What this explicitly rejects (from the Option A proposal): raw-keyword-only free search (loses to free incumbents, kills the funnel); locked eligibility teaser cards on free results (the bait-and-blur pattern, rejected twice now); a free tier designed to generate admin load as conversion pressure (inverts the load-reduction principle); and the claim that the MCP is keyword-only (the test captures prove it does eligibility-aware judgment — the free web tier must not be dumber than the free MCP).

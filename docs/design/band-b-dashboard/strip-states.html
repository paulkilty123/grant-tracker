You're right — the light style should win everywhere

I argued the panel should go deep when something is urgent. Measuring it says otherwise: the deep version destroys the colour system it contains. One container style, urgency carried by the slot and the header instead.

Why the deep version was the wrong call

On a deep panel the tinted slots don't stay tints — they composite against #1D3C3E and come out as near-identical dark greys:

SlotIntendedWhat it actually renders asSeparation from panel
Urgentterracotta#3B45421.20:1

Three hues in, three muddy greys out, none of them separable from the panel or from each other. The icon tiles still carry colour, but the slots themselves stop meaning anything. I'd just spent a pass adding colour and then put it on a surface that eats it.

On white the same three tints give 8.8–10.5:1 for deep text and stay recognisably terracotta, sage and gold.

One more reason, unrelated to contrast

Your sidebar is --deep and 250px of it runs the full height of every page. A deep strip immediately to its right makes two large dark masses meeting at a corner, and the page's centre of gravity ends up in the top-left rather than on the content. The white strip keeps the deep for what it now means everywhere else: the sidebar, primary buttons, and the "Won" pipeline tile.

What this costs: the escalation is quieter than a full colour inversion. I think that's correct — the strip appears on every visit, and a panel that transforms whenever anything is due teaches people to stop reading it. A top rule, a flag and one terracotta slot is a step change you notice without one that you tune out.
